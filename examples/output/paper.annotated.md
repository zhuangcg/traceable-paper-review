## Library hours and visits — FICTIONAL DEMO

### Background

<mark style="background-color:#a6cee3">This document is an entirely fictional demonstration, not a scientific study.</mark> [(E0001)](notes.md#e0001)

<mark style="background-color:#a6cee3">The example asks whether libraries with longer opening hours also record more visits.</mark> [(E0002)](notes.md#e0002)

<mark style="background-color:#a6cee3">A relationship between opening hours and visits would not establish causation.</mark> [(E0003)](notes.md#e0003)

### Methods

<mark style="background-color:#b2df8a">The fictional dataset contains twelve libraries observed during one illustrative week.</mark> [(E0004)](notes.md#e0004)

<mark style="background-color:#b2df8a">Six libraries are placed in a shorter-hours group and six in a longer-hours group.</mark> [(E0005)](notes.md#e0005)

<mark style="background-color:#b2df8a">The method compares the average number of visits between the two groups.</mark> [(E0006)](notes.md#e0006)

### Results

<mark style="background-color:#ffcf78">The shorter-hours group has an illustrative mean of 120 visits per library.</mark> [(E0007)](notes.md#e0007)

<mark style="background-color:#ffcf78">The longer-hours group has an illustrative mean of 180 visits per library.</mark> [(E0008)](notes.md#e0008)

<mark style="background-color:#ffcf78">The difference between the two illustrative group means is 60 visits.</mark> [(E0009)](notes.md#e0009)

### Discussion

<mark style="background-color:#cbb5e8">Library size and neighborhood population could explain part of the observed difference.</mark> [(E0010)](notes.md#e0010)

<mark style="background-color:#cbb5e8">The example does not measure uncertainty or test the effects of changing opening hours.</mark> [(E0011)](notes.md#e0011)
